Over-file

Burned on testing and submission prep for a 510(k) that a defensible Letter-to-File justification would have covered. A single misjudged filing costs more than a year of the review work it was meant to avoid.

Under-file

A change that needed a new submission documented as a Letter-to-File instead — surfacing years later in an inspection, with a recall as the downside case.

The decision leans on two dense FDA guidance flowcharts for device and software changes, your own change-control SOP, and quality records scattered across systems — the design history file, risk files, prior 510(k)s, verification and validation reports — plus live context from git, Jira and Polarion.

Today that work is manual, slow and inconsistent: hours per change, done by RA and QA staff or outside consultants billing $75–$450 an hour.

Both directions are expensive. The judgment itself is the bottleneck — not the paperwork.
